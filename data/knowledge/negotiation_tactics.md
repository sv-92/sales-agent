# Negotiation Tactics and Closing Strategies

## Closing Frameworks

### The Assumptive Close
When the prospect has shown strong buying signals, proceed as if the decision is made:
- "Should we start with the standard or premium tier?"
- "Would you like implementation to begin this month or next?"
- "I'll send over the contract — should it go to you or procurement?"

### The Summary Close
Recap all agreed-upon value and ask for commitment:
1. Summarize the key problems you've identified together
2. Map each problem to your solution's capability
3. Restate the ROI or business case
4. Ask a direct closing question

### The Urgency Close (Use Sparingly)
Only when genuine time pressure exists:
- End-of-quarter pricing that expires
- Implementation slots filling up
- Competitor momentum in their organization
- Business events creating natural deadlines

## Negotiation Principles

### Never Concede Without Getting Something Back
- If they want a discount → ask for a longer contract term
- If they want extra features → ask for a case study commitment
- If they want faster delivery → ask for expedited decision timeline
- If they want extended payment terms → ask for upfront annual commitment

### Anchoring Strategy
- Always present your full-price option first
- Package comparisons should make the middle tier most attractive
- Use specific numbers (not round) — $47,500 feels more calculated than $50,000
- Reference the value delivered, not just the cost

### Handling "Last Minute" Discount Requests
1. Express that the price reflects the value discussed
2. Ask what changed since they agreed this was a good fit
3. If discount is necessary, remove scope rather than reducing price
4. Offer creative alternatives: longer term, more seats, training credits

## Stalled Deal Revival

### Signs a Deal is Stalling
- Emails go unanswered for 5+ days
- Meetings get rescheduled multiple times
- New stakeholders suddenly appear
- "We need more time to think about it"

### Revival Tactics
1. **The breakup email**: "It seems like timing isn't right. Should I close this out?"
2. **New information**: Share a relevant case study or product update
3. **Executive outreach**: Have your leadership reach out to their leadership
4. **Event-based**: Invite them to a webinar, dinner, or industry event
5. **Peer reference**: Connect them with a happy customer in their industry

## Multi-Stakeholder Deals

### Managing Multiple Decision Makers
- Map the org chart: identify champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, end users
- Customize messaging for each stakeholder's priorities
- Keep your champion informed and armed with internal selling materials
- Address blockers individually rather than in group settings
