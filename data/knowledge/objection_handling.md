# Objection Handling Playbook

## Pricing Objections

### "Your solution is too expensive"

**Framework: Value-Based Response**

1. **Acknowledge** the concern without being defensive. Budget is always a valid consideration.
2. **Reframe** the conversation from cost to value and ROI.
3. **Quantify** the business impact of the problem they're solving.

**Key Tactics:**

- Calculate the cost of inaction: "What does this problem cost you per month in lost revenue/productivity?"
- Compare to alternatives: total cost of ownership, not just license fees
- Break down per-user or per-transaction costs to make the number feel smaller
- Offer phased rollout to reduce initial commitment

**Enterprise-Specific Approach:**

For enterprise customers, pricing objections often mask other concerns:
- They may need internal justification for budget approval
- Provide an ROI calculator or business case template they can present to their CFO
- Offer a pilot program with measurable success criteria
- Reference similar-sized companies and their realized ROI

### "Competitor X is cheaper"

**Framework: Differentiation Response**

1. Never disparage the competitor directly
2. Ask what's included in the competitor's quote — often it's apples to oranges
3. Highlight unique capabilities that competitors lack
4. Emphasize total cost of ownership including implementation, training, and ongoing support

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- "What specific capabilities are they offering at that price point?"
- "Does their quote include implementation and training?"
- "Have you talked to their existing customers about hidden costs?"

## Timeline Objections

### "We're not ready to buy right now"

**Response Strategy:**
- Understand their timeline and what's driving it
- Identify the trigger event that would make them ready
- Offer value in the interim (content, workshops, assessments)
- Set a specific follow-up date with clear next steps

## Authority Objections

### "I need to check with my team/boss"

**Response Strategy:**
- Ask who else is involved in the decision
- Offer to present to the broader group
- Provide materials they can share internally
- Ask what criteria their decision-maker uses
