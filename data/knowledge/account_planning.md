# Account Planning and Territory Management

## Territory Planning Framework

### Segmentation Criteria
Organize accounts by potential and fit:

| Segment | Revenue Potential | Product Fit | Action |
|---------|------------------|-------------|--------|
| A-List | >$500K ACV | High | Dedicated strategy, exec sponsorship |
| B-List | $100K-$500K ACV | High | Structured outreach, quarterly reviews |
| C-List | $50K-$100K ACV | Medium | Scalable campaigns, pooled resources |
| D-List | <$50K ACV | Low | Self-service, partner-led |

### Quarterly Planning Cadence
1. **Week 1**: Analyze last quarter's performance vs targets
2. **Week 2**: Update account tiering based on new data
3. **Week 3**: Build 90-day action plans for Tier 1 accounts
4. **Week 4**: Align resources and get manager approval

## Account Health Scoring

### Green (Healthy)
- Active engagement (meetings in last 30 days)
- Champion identified and responsive
- Pipeline opportunities progressing through stages
- No competitive threats detected

### Yellow (At Risk)
- Engagement gap (30-60 days since last meeting)
- Champion role changed or went silent
- Deals stuck in same stage for 2+ weeks
- Competitor mentioned in conversations

### Red (Critical)
- No engagement for 60+ days
- Lost champion with no replacement
- Deals pushed multiple times
- Active competitive evaluation underway

## Expansion Planning

### Identifying Expansion Opportunities
Look for these signals in existing accounts:
- New department hires (growing team = growing need)
- Strategic initiatives announced (digital transformation, M&A)
- Usage metrics approaching tier limits
- Support tickets from new teams (organic spread)
- Executive changes (new leaders bring new priorities)

### The QBR (Quarterly Business Review)
Structure for maximum expansion opportunity:
1. **Value delivered** — show metrics improvement since purchase
2. **Usage insights** — highlight adoption trends and power users
3. **Roadmap preview** — tease upcoming features relevant to them
4. **Growth discussion** — "What other teams could benefit from this?"
5. **Success stories** — share how similar companies expanded

## Forecasting Best Practices

### Commit vs Best Case vs Pipeline
- **Commit**: Deals you would bet your job on closing this quarter (90%+ confidence)
- **Best Case**: Deals with strong signals that could close with effort (60-80%)
- **Pipeline**: All active deals regardless of stage (weighted by stage probability)

### Stage-Based Probability
| Stage | Probability | Criteria |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Prospecting | 10% | Initial interest confirmed |
| Qualification | 25% | MEDDIC criteria partially met |
| Proposal | 50% | Solution presented, pricing discussed |
| Negotiation | 75% | Terms being finalized |
| Closed Won | 100% | Contract signed |
| Closed Lost | 0% | Deal lost to competitor or no-decision |

### Forecast Accuracy Tips
- Never count a deal as commit without verbal confirmation from the buyer
- Track forecast accuracy over time to calibrate your own optimism
- Update forecasts weekly, not just at quarter end
- Flag deals that move backward in stage (they rarely recover)
